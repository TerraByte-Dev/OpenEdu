# P3 — does a purpose-built unit clear the pool law by construction?

**Date:** 2026-09-08 · **Verdict: YES, by a wide margin.** · Reproduce: `npm test -- src/lib/steps`

F0(b) failed on the card tier — 30.5% of cards bear mastery against a 40% bar. Everything then
rested on one untested claim: that a unit *written to be harvestable* clears the pool law by
construction. If it did not, the content-factory premise was in serious trouble.

## Measured

Two units hand-authored to the `build-units.mjs` spec, compiled by the shipped
`src/lib/steps/` with **zero model calls**:

| | harvested | kept | closed-book | kinds | bears mastery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `living-skills/navigation/map-and-compass` | 52 | **52** | **52** | 2 | **yes** |
| `living-skills/finance/borrowing-basics` | — | — | ≥10 | 2 | **yes** |

**Zero gate rejections.** V0, V1, V2 and V3 rejected nothing — authored-to-spec content does not
trip them, which is what you want: the gates exist to catch generated content, not to fight authors.

## The contrast, which is the actual result

| | closed-book items |
|---|---|
| Average shipped **card** | ~0 — and **119 of 154 yield nothing at all** |
| One authored **unit** | **52** |
| Pool law requires | 10 |

A card is 176 words of reference prose. A unit is the same length with its facts arranged in tables
and definition lists, and that single difference is worth 52 items to 0. The harvest-surface rule —
every concept section ends in a table of ≥4 rows with ≥8 distinct short answers, or a definition
list of ≥8 entries — is therefore a **build gate in `build-units.mjs`, not a style note.** A unit
that does not satisfy it fails the build rather than silently shipping something the runtime cannot
teach.

## What this does and does not settle

**Settles:** the corpus-to-assessment premise is sound at the unit tier. The compiler works, the
gates work, and a human writing to the spec produces a mastery-bearing concept without effort. The
factory has a target format that is known-achievable.

**Does not settle:** whether a *model* can hit the spec as reliably as a human did here, which is the
whole content-factory bet and needs its own measurement. Nor whether these items *teach* — 52
recall items over a compass rose is a drill, and the design's own warning stands: "a full mastery
dashboard over 31 factual concepts must never read as 'general education'."

## A note on scope

These two units are navigation and finance, not first aid. First aid is `harm_class: high`, which
the builder requires to be sourced entirely from `pd_source`, `canonical` or `computed` claims with
real URLs — and I will not fabricate MedlinePlus links to satisfy a gate whose entire purpose is to
stop exactly that. P3 tests the **format**; the first-aid unit is blocked on verified sourcing, not
on format, and that is a separate task.

## Three gate bugs this found

Authoring the first unit against the builder immediately exposed three flaws in my own gates:

1. **Verbatim claim matching failed on wrapped prose.** Bodies are hard-wrapped at ~100 columns, so
   any claim spanning a line break could never match. Now compares whitespace-collapsed.
2. **`computed` claims were required to carry a `source_url`.** They are verified by re-deriving
   them; a `derivation` *is* the evidence. Now required instead.
3. **`canonical` claims were required to carry a `source_url`.** A standard convention may have no
   stable URL; a named `authority` is the right evidence. Now either satisfies.

None would have surfaced without writing real content against the real gate.
