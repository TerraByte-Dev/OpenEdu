# k12-flow (attempt-first) — lever L1 A/B — `gemma4:e2b`, `gemma4:e4b`, `qwen3.5:4b` — dev — 2026-11-07

> **DEV, not a test result.** These are the 240 k12-mc items, which are DEV for this flow
> (`docs/BENCHMARKS.md` section 0). Their rows were read for this file. Do not quote these numbers as
> a benchmark result. The detectors have not passed the hand-label check (κ ≥ 0.8), so nothing here is
> frozen.

Lever L1 (`docs/BENCHMARKS.md` section 3): on the turn whose user message is exactly
`SHOW_ANSWER_MESSAGE`, the kernel swaps the output rules for `WORKED_ANSWER_RULES`
(`src/lib/kernel/outputRules.ts`). PR #184, issue #183. Prompt-wording iteration **1 of 3**. The
wording was written before any run and not changed after.

> **Gate fix after review.** In the three L1 arms below (commit `775ee0e`) the swap fired on the chip's
> text alone, so every T3 got the worked-answer rules, including the 23, 50 and 223 items (e2b, e4b,
> qwen3.5:4b) where the product would not have offered the chip. Commit `1c404a9` swaps only when the
> chip was offered under the reply before. Those arms therefore overstate what the product does on
> non-chip items. The chip-offered breakdown and an e2b confirmation run at `1c404a9` are in
> [After the review](#after-the-review-chip-gate-fix-and-confirmation-run). The earlier numbers are
> kept as run.

The kill rule was posted in PR #184 before the first run: kill if, on any model, G1 drops by more than
2 points, or no-commit at T3 (G5 fail) is not at least halved.

## Verdict

**The kill rule does not fire on any model.** No-commit at T3 fell from 99 to 0 (e2b), 160 to 3 (e4b)
and 71 to 2 (qwen3.5:4b). G1 moved −1.2, +5.0 and −0.8 points. G3 rose by 40, 65 and 29 points, each
with exact McNemar p < 1e-18. The lever helps on two model families and hurts neither beyond its
threshold, so it counts as general on dev (rule 10).

## Kill rule

| | gemma4:e2b | gemma4:e4b | qwen3.5:4b |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 baseline → L1 | 163 → 160 (−1.2 pts) | 95 → 107 (+5.0 pts) | 13 → 11 (−0.8 pts) |

| Fires? | no | no | no |

T1's request is byte-identical between arms (`turnRules.test.ts`, `parity.test.ts`), and Ollama
samples without a fixed seed. So the G1 changes are sampling noise. None is significant (McNemar
p = 0.76, 0.14, 0.82). The same holds for G1b, G2, G4 and the chip, which depend on T1 and T2 only.

So for this lever, which changes T3 only, the G1 clause measured noise and nothing else. Given the
discordant pairs here (41, 56, 20), a bare 2-point drop fires on noise alone with probability about
0.27, 0.25 and 0.13, so about one in two that it fires on at least one model. The verdict above stands
as pre-registered. A significance condition for future T3-only levers is proposed, not applied
retroactively, in `docs/BENCHMARKS.md` section 3.

## After the review: chip gate fix and confirmation run

### The gate (review finding 1)

At `775ee0e` the kernel swapped the rules whenever the newest user message was the chip's text. Typed
or pasted as a first message, it got a full reveal under rules that say the student tried. From
`1c404a9` the swap also needs the chip to have been offered under the reply before:
`offersShowAnswer` is the chip's one rule, called by `suggestFollowUps` (ChatTab's chips) and, through
`showAnswerOfferedAfter`, by the kernel on the saved conversation. The k12-flow driver now records the
kernel's gate per row (`workedAnswerTurn`).

Offline proof before the confirmation run: over every row of the six runs above (baseline and L1,
three models, 1,440 rows), the new gate evaluated on the row's saved T1/T2 history equals the row's
recorded `chipOffered` on **1440 of 1440** (223, 204, 19, 217, 190 and 17 offered). No T1 or T2 reply
in those runs stopped early, so the stopped and length-cut paths of the gate are covered by unit tests
only, not by this data.
