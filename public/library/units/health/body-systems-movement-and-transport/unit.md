# Body Systems: Movement and Transport

The body is built in layers, from tiny cells up to whole systems. This unit covers the systems that
hold you up, move you, and carry oxygen and food to every part of you.

## Levels of organisation

The cell is the smallest living unit of the body; every living thing is made of cells. A tissue is a
large group of similar cells that work together. An organ, like the heart or a lung, is made of
several different kinds of tissue working together for one job. An organ system is a team of organs
that work together, and it is the most complex level inside the body. The whole person, made of all
the systems together, is called the organism.

The body has four main kinds of tissue: epithelial (covering and lining), connective, muscle, and
nervous.

Books do not all count the organ systems the same way. NCI SEER lists ten major organ systems, and
other books group them differently, so learn the levels rather than a number.

- **Cell** — the smallest living unit of the body
- **Tissue** — a large group of similar cells that work together
- **Organ** — several different kinds of tissue working together for one job
- **Organ system** — a team of organs working together, the most complex level inside the body
- **Organism** — the whole person, made of all the organ systems together
- **Epithelial tissue** — the main kind of tissue that covers and lines the body
- **Heart** — an organ, made of several kinds of tissue, that pumps blood
- **Lung** — an organ, made of several kinds of tissue, where oxygen moves into the blood

## Skeleton and joints

The skeleton is a hard frame that holds the body up and protects soft organs like the brain, heart
and lungs. Most new blood cells are made inside bones, in the soft red marrow. An adult skeleton
usually has 206 named bones.

A joint is a place where two bones meet; joints can be immovable, slightly movable, or freely
movable. Most joints in an adult body, like the knee or elbow, are freely movable.

- **Skeleton** — the hard frame of bones that holds the body up and protects soft organs
- **Red marrow** — the soft part inside bones where most new blood cells are made
- **Blood cells** — what the red marrow inside bones mostly makes
- **206** — the usual number of named bones in an adult skeleton
- **Joint** — a place where two bones meet
- **Immovable joint** — a joint that does not move at all
- **Slightly movable joint** — a joint that allows only a little movement
- **Freely movable joint** — the most common kind of joint in an adult, such as the knee or elbow

## Muscles

Skeletal muscle is attached to bones and moves the body, and you control it on purpose (it is
voluntary). Smooth muscle is found in the walls of hollow organs such as blood vessels, the stomach
and gut, and the bladder. It works without you thinking about it (it is involuntary). Cardiac muscle
is found in the wall of the heart. It squeezes strongly and in a steady rhythm, and you cannot
control it on purpose (it is involuntary).

A muscle usually crosses a joint and is tied to bones at each end by tendons. When it tightens, one
bone stays mostly still and the other bone moves.

- **Skeletal muscle** — muscle attached to bones that moves the body under your control
- **Smooth muscle** — muscle in the walls of hollow organs such as the stomach and the bladder
- **Cardiac muscle** — muscle in the wall of the heart that squeezes in a steady rhythm
- **Voluntary** — controlled on purpose, as skeletal muscle is
- **Involuntary** — working without you thinking about it, as smooth and cardiac muscle do
- **Tendon** — the cord that ties a muscle to a bone
- **Hollow organs** — organs such as the stomach, gut and bladder whose walls hold smooth muscle
- **Heart wall** — the place in the body where cardiac muscle is found

## Heart and blood

The heart has four chambers: two thin-walled atria on top that receive blood, and two thick-walled
ventricles below that pump blood out. Blood coming back from the body, low in oxygen, enters the right
side of the heart, which pumps it to the lungs to pick up oxygen. Blood coming back from the lungs enters the left side of the heart, which pumps it out to
the whole body.

Arteries carry blood away from the heart, and veins carry blood back toward the heart. Capillaries
are the smallest and most common blood vessels, and their main job is to swap materials between the
blood and the body's cells. Blood carries oxygen from the lungs to the body and carbon dioxide back
to the lungs, and it also carries food from digestion, hormones, germ-fighting substances, and
wastes.

For an adult at rest, a heart rate of 60 to 100 beats per minute is normal. Some adults, especially
young adults or physically fit people, may normally have slower heart rates. This range is for adults only,
so do not use it to judge a child's pulse, and never decide from one pulse reading that someone is
ill. That is a health worker's job.

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| Atria | Receive blood |
| Ventricles | Pump blood out |
| Right side of heart | Pumps blood to lungs |
| Left side of heart | Pumps blood to body |
| Arteries | Carry blood from heart |
| Veins | Carry blood to heart |
| Capillaries | Swap materials with cells |
| Lungs | Where blood picks up oxygen |

## Breathing

Air goes in through the nose or mouth, which warm and wet it, then passes the voice box and travels
down the windpipe. Inside the lungs the tubes branch into thin bronchioles that end in tiny air sacs
called alveoli, where oxygen moves into the blood in the capillaries and carbon dioxide moves out to
be breathed away.

To breathe in, the diaphragm tightens and the chest gets bigger so air flows into the lungs; to
breathe out, the diaphragm relaxes and air is pushed out.

- **Nose and mouth** — where air comes in, and where it is warmed and wetted
- **Voice box** — the part air passes just before it travels down the windpipe
- **Windpipe** — the tube air travels down on its way to the lungs
- **Bronchioles** — thin branching tubes inside the lungs that end in air sacs
- **Alveoli** — tiny air sacs in the lungs where gases pass to and from the blood
- **Oxygen** — the gas that moves from the air sacs into the blood
- **Carbon dioxide** — the gas that moves out of the blood to be breathed away
- **Diaphragm** — the muscle that tightens to breathe in and relaxes to breathe out
