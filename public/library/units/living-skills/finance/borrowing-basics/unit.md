# Borrowing Basics

Every loan is the same three numbers wearing different names: how much you borrowed, what rate you
pay, and how long you take. Everything else on the paperwork is a consequence of those three.

## Interest vocabulary

A lender's contract is precise, and the precision is the point — two loans with the same headline
rate can cost very different amounts depending on which of these words apply.

- **Principal** — the amount actually borrowed, before any interest
- **Interest** — the fee charged for the use of the principal, expressed as a rate
- **Simple interest** — interest charged only on the principal, never on interest already accrued
- **Compound interest** — interest charged on the principal plus the interest already added
- **APR** — the yearly cost of a loan including fees, which is why it can exceed the interest rate
- **Term** — how long you have to repay, usually in months or years
- **Amortisation** — the schedule by which each payment is split between interest and principal
- **Collateral** — property the lender may take if the loan is not repaid
- **Secured** — a loan backed by collateral, which is why its rate is usually lower
- **Unsecured** — a loan backed only by a promise, which is why its rate is usually higher
- **Default** — failing to meet the repayment terms
- **Refinance** — replacing a loan with a new one, usually to change the rate or the term

## Cost of credit

Doubling the term does not halve the cost — it lowers the payment and raises the total. This table
is one principal of 10,000 at one rate of 8 percent, changing only the term.
