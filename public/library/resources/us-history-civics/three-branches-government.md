# The Three Branches of Government

The U.S. Constitution splits federal power into three branches (**separation of powers**) so no one branch grows too strong.

## Legislative — Congress (Senate + House)
Makes the laws. Controls federal spending and taxes, declares war, and confirms or impeaches officials.

## Executive — President + Cabinet
Enforces the laws. The President is commander-in-chief, can veto bills, makes treaties, and appoints judges and officials.

## Judicial — Supreme Court + federal courts
Interprets the laws. Through **judicial review** (Marbury v. Madison, 1803) it can declare a law or action unconstitutional.

## Checks & balances
- Congress passes a law → the President can **veto** it → Congress can **override** with a two-thirds vote.
- The President appoints judges → the **Senate must confirm** them.
- The courts can strike down acts of Congress or the President as unconstitutional.

This system forces the branches to cooperate and keeps power balanced.
