# How a Bill Becomes a Law (Federal)

A bill must pass **both chambers of Congress in identical form** and be signed by the President (or have a veto overridden).

## The steps
1. **Introduction.** A member introduces the bill in the House or Senate. (Revenue/tax bills must start in the House — Art. I, §7.)
2. **Committee.** Referred to a standing committee → often a subcommittee. Hearings, **markup** (edits), then it's reported out, tabled, or it dies.
3. **Floor debate & vote.** The full chamber debates and votes. A **simple majority** passes it.
4. **Other chamber.** The second chamber repeats steps 2–3. It may amend the bill.
5. **Reconciliation.** If the two versions differ, a **conference committee** merges them; both chambers must pass the identical final text.
6. **President.** Three options:
   - **Sign** → it becomes law.
   - **Veto** → returned to Congress with objections.
   - **No action for 10 days** (Sundays excepted): becomes law if Congress is in session; **pocket veto** (dies) if Congress has adjourned.
7. **Override.** Congress can override a veto with a **two-thirds vote in both chambers** → it becomes law without the President.

Most bills die in committee; only a small fraction become law.
