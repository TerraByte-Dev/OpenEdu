# Circular & Rotational Motion

## Uniform circular motion
Moving in a circle at constant speed, the **direction** keeps changing — so the object is **accelerating toward the center**.

- **Centripetal acceleration:**  a_c = v² / r = ω²·r
- **Centripetal force:**  F_c = m·v² / r   (always points to the center)
- **Period:**  T = 2π·r / v

There is no outward "centrifugal force" — your inertia makes you feel flung outward, but the real force points **inward**.

## Rotational analogs of linear motion
| Linear | Rotational |
|---|---|
| displacement x | angle θ (radians) |
| velocity v | angular velocity ω |
| acceleration a | angular acceleration α |
| force F | torque τ = r·F·sin φ |
| mass m | moment of inertia I |
| **F = m·a** | **τ = I·α** |

In torque, **φ is the angle between r and F** (not the rotation angle θ); torque is greatest when **F ⊥ r**.

**Links (θ = rotation angle, in radians):** arc length s = r·θ, speed v = r·ω, acceleration a = r·α.
