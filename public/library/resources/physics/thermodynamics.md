# Thermodynamics

The physics of **heat** and energy transfer.

## The four laws
- **0th:** if A is in thermal equilibrium with B, and B with C, then A with C — this defines temperature.
- **1st:** **ΔU = Q − W** — energy is conserved (internal-energy change = heat added − work done by the system).
- **2nd:** heat flows **hot → cold** on its own; the **entropy** of an isolated system never decreases.
- **3rd:** as temperature → **absolute zero (0 K)**, entropy approaches a minimum; 0 K is unreachable.

## Heat & temperature
- **Warm a substance:**  Q = m·c·ΔT   (c = specific heat)
- **Phase change:**  Q = m·L   (L = latent heat; no temperature change)
- **Kelvin:**  K = °C + 273.15    **Fahrenheit:**  °F = (9/5)·°C + 32

**Heat transfer:** conduction (contact), convection (fluid flow), radiation (EM waves).

**Example:** heating 0.5 kg of water (c = 4186 J/kg·°C) by 20 °C needs Q = 0.5 × 4186 × 20 ≈ 41,900 J. (For gas behavior, see the chemistry *Gas Laws* card.)
