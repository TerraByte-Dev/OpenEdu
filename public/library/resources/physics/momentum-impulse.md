# Momentum & Impulse

**Momentum** is mass in motion — a vector. **Impulse** is the change in momentum.

- **Momentum:**  p = m·v   (units kg·m/s)
- **Impulse:**  J = F·Δt = Δp = m·Δv   (units N·s)
- **Newton's 2nd law (general form):**  F = Δp / Δt

## Conservation of momentum
In an **isolated system** (no external net force), total momentum is conserved:

  Σp (before) = Σp (after)  →  m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

This is why a gun recoils and a rocket accelerates.

## Collisions
- **Elastic:** momentum **and** kinetic energy conserved — objects bounce apart.
- **Inelastic:** momentum conserved, kinetic energy is **not** (lost to heat/sound).
- **Perfectly inelastic:** objects stick together and move as one.

**Example:** a 10 N force acts for 0.5 s → J = 10 × 0.5 = 5 N·s, so the object's momentum changes by 5 kg·m/s. A 2 kg cart at 3 m/s has p = 6 kg·m/s.
