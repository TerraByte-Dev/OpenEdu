# Strong Acids & Strong Bases

A **strong** acid or base ionizes ~100% in water. The lists are short — **memorize them; assume anything else is weak.**

**The 7 strong acids**
- HCl — hydrochloric
- HBr — hydrobromic
- HI — hydroiodic
- HNO₃ — nitric
- H₂SO₄ — sulfuric (first proton)
- HClO₄ — perchloric
- HClO₃ — chloric

**Strong bases** (soluble metal hydroxides)
- Group 1: LiOH, NaOH, KOH, RbOH, CsOH
- Heavy Group 2: Ca(OH)₂, Sr(OH)₂, Ba(OH)₂

**Examples of WEAK acids/bases:** acetic acid (CH₃COOH / vinegar), carbonic acid (H₂CO₃), phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄), HF, and ammonia (NH₃, a weak base).

Strong ≠ concentrated: "strong" is **how completely** it ionizes; "concentrated" is **how much** is dissolved. A strong acid produces a low pH because it releases nearly all its H⁺.
