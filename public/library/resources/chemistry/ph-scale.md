# The pH Scale

pH measures how acidic or basic a water solution is, from **0 to 14**.

- **pH < 7 — acidic** (more H⁺ ions)
- **pH = 7 — neutral** (pure water)
- **pH > 7 — basic / alkaline** (more OH⁻ ions)

## The math
- **pH = −log[H⁺]** (where [H⁺] is the hydrogen-ion concentration in mol/L)
- **pOH = −log[OH⁻]**, and **pH + pOH = 14**

Each whole pH unit is a **10× change** in acidity: pH 3 is 10 times more acidic than pH 4, and 100 times more than pH 5.

## Everyday examples
- 0 battery acid · 1 stomach acid · 2 lemon juice · 3 vinegar
- 5 black coffee · 6 milk · **7 pure water**
- 8 seawater · 9 baking soda · 11 ammonia · 13 bleach · 14 lye (drain cleaner)

Strong acids (HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃) fully ionize, giving very low pH; strong bases (NaOH, KOH) give very high pH.
