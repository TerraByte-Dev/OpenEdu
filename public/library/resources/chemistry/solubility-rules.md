# Solubility Rules

Rules for whether an ionic compound dissolves in water (aqueous). Apply the soluble rules first; exceptions override.

## Generally SOLUBLE
- All salts of Group 1 metals (Li, Na, K, etc.) and ammonium (NH4+).
- All nitrates (NO3-), acetates (C2H3O2-), and chlorates (ClO3-).
- Chlorides, bromides, iodides — EXCEPT those of Ag+, Pb2+, and Hg2(2+).
- Sulfates (SO4 2-) — EXCEPT those of Ba2+, Pb2+, Ca2+, Sr2+, and Ag+.

## Generally INSOLUBLE
- Carbonates (CO3 2-), phosphates (PO4 3-), and sulfides (S2-) — EXCEPT with Group 1 metals and ammonium.
- Hydroxides (OH-) — EXCEPT with Group 1 metals (soluble); Ca2+, Sr2+, Ba2+ are slightly soluble.
- Most oxides — EXCEPT with Group 1 metals.

## How to use
When two soluble solutions are mixed, swap the ions: if a new pair is insoluble, it forms a solid **precipitate**. Example: AgNO3 + NaCl forms AgCl, a white precipitate (silver chloride is insoluble).
