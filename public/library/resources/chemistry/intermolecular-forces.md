# Intermolecular Forces (IMFs)

Attractions **between** molecules (not the covalent bonds within them). They are weaker than bonds but set melting/boiling points, solubility, and surface tension. Ranked weakest → strongest:

1. **London dispersion forces** — present in **all** molecules. Caused by momentary, random electron shifts creating temporary dipoles. Stronger in bigger molecules with more electrons. The only IMF in nonpolar molecules (N₂, CH₄, noble gases).
2. **Dipole–dipole** — between **polar** molecules; the positive end of one attracts the negative end of another (HCl, acetone).
3. **Hydrogen bonding** — a strong special dipole–dipole: an H bonded to **N, O, or F** is attracted to a lone pair on N/O/F of another molecule (water, NH₃, DNA, proteins).

**Why it matters:** stronger IMFs → higher boiling point. Water boils far higher than similar-size molecules because of hydrogen bonding; that bonding also gives water its high surface tension and lets ice float.

Ion–dipole forces (e.g. dissolving NaCl in water) are even stronger but involve ions, not neutral molecules.
