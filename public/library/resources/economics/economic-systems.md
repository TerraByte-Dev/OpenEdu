# Types of Economic Systems

Every economy must answer three questions: **what** to produce, **how** to produce it, and **for whom**. Systems differ by who decides.

| System | Who decides | Who owns resources | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Traditional** | custom & habit | family / community | subsistence farming and hunter-gatherer societies |
| **Command** | the central government | the government (state) | former USSR, North Korea, Cuba |
| **Market** (capitalism) | buyers & sellers (supply & demand) | private individuals | the idealized free market |
| **Mixed** | both markets and government | private and public | **most countries today** (U.S., U.K., Japan…) |

- **Market** economies reward efficiency and choice but can leave gaps (the poor, public goods).
- **Command** economies aim for equality and stability but often lack incentives and variety.
- **Mixed** economies blend private markets with government rules and services — the real-world norm.
