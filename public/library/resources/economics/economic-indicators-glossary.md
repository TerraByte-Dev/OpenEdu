# Economic Indicators Glossary

The headline numbers used to judge an economy's health.

- **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)** — the total market value of all goods and services produced **within a country** in a year. The main measure of economic size and growth.
- **GNP (Gross National Product)** — output by a country's **residents/citizens**, wherever they produce it.
- **GDP per capita** — GDP ÷ population; a rough measure of living standards.
- **Inflation** — a general **rise in prices** over time, which lowers money's buying power. Tracked by the **CPI (Consumer Price Index)**, a basket of common goods.
- **Unemployment rate** — the % of the **labor force** that is jobless but actively seeking work.
- **Interest rate** — the cost of borrowing money, influenced by a central bank (e.g., the Federal Reserve).
- **Recession** — a significant, sustained decline in economic activity (commonly two straight quarters of falling GDP).

**Policy levers:** **fiscal policy** = government taxing and spending; **monetary policy** = a central bank adjusting the money supply and interest rates.
