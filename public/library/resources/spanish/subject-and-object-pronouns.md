# Spanish Pronouns

| Person | Subject | Direct obj. | Indirect obj. | Reflexive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1s | yo | me | me | me |
| 2s | tú | te | te | te |
| 3s | él/ella/usted | lo / la | le | se |
| 1pl | nosotros/-as | nos | nos | nos |
| 2pl | vosotros/-as | os | os | os |
| 3pl | ellos/ellas/ustedes | los / las | les | se |

## How they work
- **Subject** pronouns are usually **dropped** — the verb ending shows the person (*Hablo.* = I speak). Use them for emphasis or contrast.
- **Direct object** = receives the action (*Lo veo* = I see it/him). *lo/la/los/las* agree in gender and number.
- **Indirect object** = to/for whom (*Le doy el libro* = I give the book to him/her).
- **Reflexive** = subject acts on itself (*Me lavo* = I wash myself); used with reflexive verbs (lavarse, levantarse).

## Placement
- Before a conjugated verb: *Te llamo.*
- Attached to an infinitive, gerund, or affirmative command: *Voy a llamarte. Llámame.*
- Two pronouns: **indirect before direct** (*Me lo da*). When both are 3rd person, **le/les → se** before lo/la/los/las (*se lo doy*, not "le lo").

**tú** = informal you; **usted** = formal you (takes 3rd-person verbs). *vosotros* (Spain) vs *ustedes* (Latin America) for plural "you."
