# Spanish Articles & Noun Gender

Every Spanish noun is **masculine or feminine**, and its article + adjectives must **agree** in gender and number.

## The articles
| | masculine sing. | feminine sing. | masc. plural | fem. plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **definite** (the) | el | la | los | las |
| **indefinite** (a/some) | un | una | unos | unas |

*el libro, la mesa, los libros, unas mesas.*

## Telling gender from the ending
- Usually **masculine:** nouns ending in **-o** (el libro), plus -or, -aje, -ma (from Greek).
- Usually **feminine:** nouns ending in **-a** (la casa), plus -ción, -sión, -dad, -tad, -tud, -umbre.

## Common exceptions
- Masculine despite -a: **el día, el mapa, el problema, el clima, el sofá, el planeta.**
- Feminine despite -o: **la mano, la foto, la moto, la radio.**

## Making plurals
- Ends in a vowel → add **-s**: casa → casas.
- Ends in a consonant → add **-es**: papel → papeles. (A final -z becomes -c: lápiz → lápices.)

**el → un, la → una.** Before a stressed *a-/ha-*, a feminine noun takes **el/un** for sound (*el agua, un águila*) but stays feminine (*el agua fría*).
