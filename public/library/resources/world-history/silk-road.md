# The Silk Road & Trade Routes

The **Silk Road** was a network of overland and sea routes that connected **East Asia to the Mediterranean** from about **200 BCE to 1450 CE**, peaking under the Han and Tang dynasties and again under the Mongol Empire (the "Pax Mongolica").

## The overland route (west from China)
**Chang'an (Xi'an) → Dunhuang → Kashgar → Samarkand → Merv → Baghdad → Damascus → Constantinople** (then on to Venice and Europe). Goods moved relay-style between merchants; few travelers crossed the whole length.

## The maritime "spice route"
By sea: **Guangzhou → Malacca → Calicut**, then west by one of two branches — the **Red Sea** (via **Aden**, up the Red Sea, then overland to **Alexandria**) or the **Persian Gulf** (via **Hormuz** to Basra, then overland to the Levant). The sea route grew dominant after ~1000 CE.

## What was traded
- **East → West:** silk, porcelain, tea, paper, gunpowder, spices.
- **West → East:** gold, silver, horses, wool, glassware.

## Why it mattered (beyond goods)
- Spread **ideas, technology, and religions** — especially **Buddhism** into China, and later Islam across Central Asia.
- Spread **disease**: the **Black Death** (1340s) traveled these routes.
- Its decline (Ottoman control of the land routes after 1453) pushed Europe to seek **sea routes to Asia** — launching the Age of Exploration.
