# Unit Circle

The unit circle has radius 1 centered at the origin. For an angle θ measured counterclockwise from the
positive x-axis, the point on the circle is **(cos θ, sin θ)**, and **tan θ = sin θ / cos θ**.

Format: **degrees (radians): cos, sin, tan**

- 0° (0): cos 1, sin 0, tan 0
- 30° (π/6): cos √3/2, sin 1/2, tan √3/3
- 45° (π/4): cos √2/2, sin √2/2, tan 1
- 60° (π/3): cos 1/2, sin √3/2, tan √3
- 90° (π/2): cos 0, sin 1, tan undefined
- 120° (2π/3): cos −1/2, sin √3/2, tan −√3
- 135° (3π/4): cos −√2/2, sin √2/2, tan −1
- 150° (5π/6): cos −√3/2, sin 1/2, tan −√3/3
- 180° (π): cos −1, sin 0, tan 0
- 210° (7π/6): cos −√3/2, sin −1/2, tan √3/3
- 225° (5π/4): cos −√2/2, sin −√2/2, tan 1
- 240° (4π/3): cos −1/2, sin −√3/2, tan √3
- 270° (3π/2): cos 0, sin −1, tan undefined
- 300° (5π/3): cos 1/2, sin −√3/2, tan −√3
- 315° (7π/4): cos √2/2, sin −√2/2, tan −1
- 330° (11π/6): cos √3/2, sin −1/2, tan −√3/3
- 360° (2π): same as 0°

## Key facts
- **Quadrant signs** (cos, sin): I (+,+), II (−,+), III (−,−), IV (+,−). Memory aid: "All Students Take Calculus" — All positive in QI, Sine in QII, Tangent in QIII, Cosine in QIV.
- **Radians ↔ degrees:** radians = degrees × π/180; degrees = radians × 180/π. A full turn is 360° = 2π.
- **Pythagorean identity:** sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 (because the point lies on a radius-1 circle).
