# Musical Intervals

An **interval** is the distance between two pitches, measured in **half steps** (semitones). Within one octave:

| Half steps | Interval | Short |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Perfect unison | P1 |
| 1 | Minor 2nd | m2 |
| 2 | Major 2nd | M2 |
| 3 | Minor 3rd | m3 |
| 4 | Major 3rd | M3 |
| 5 | Perfect 4th | P4 |
| 6 | Tritone (aug 4 / dim 5) | TT |
| 7 | Perfect 5th | P5 |
| 8 | Minor 6th | m6 |
| 9 | Major 6th | M6 |
| 10 | Minor 7th | m7 |
| 11 | Major 7th | M7 |
| 12 | Perfect octave | P8 |

## Key ideas
- **Quality** names: **Perfect** (unison, 4th, 5th, octave), **Major/minor** (2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th).
- A **half step** = the closest two notes (e.g. E–F, B–C, or any note to the next key); a **whole step** = 2 half steps.
- Raising a major/perfect interval by a half step = **augmented**; lowering a **perfect/minor** interval by a half step = **diminished**. (Lowering a *major* interval a half step makes it **minor**.)
- **Consonant** (stable): unisons, 3rds, 5ths, 6ths, octaves. **Dissonant** (tense): 2nds, 7ths, the tritone.
