# Layers of the Earth

By **composition**, Earth has three layers; the core splits into two by **state**. Depths are below the surface (Earth's radius ≈ 6,371 km).

- **Crust** — 0 to ~5–70 km. Thin, solid, rocky outer shell. Oceanic crust (~5–10 km, basalt) is thinner than continental crust (~30–70 km, granite).
- **Mantle** — ~70 to 2,890 km. Hot, dense silicate rock. Mostly solid but slowly flows (convection). The upper, partly molten **asthenosphere** lets tectonic plates move. The rigid crust + uppermost mantle form the **lithosphere**.
- **Outer core** — 2,890 to 5,150 km. **Liquid** iron and nickel; its motion generates Earth's **magnetic field**.
- **Inner core** — 5,150 to 6,371 km. **Solid** iron-nickel (kept solid by immense pressure despite ~5,200 °C).

Temperature and pressure rise with depth. By **mechanical** behavior the layers are: lithosphere, asthenosphere, mesosphere (lower mantle), outer core, inner core.
