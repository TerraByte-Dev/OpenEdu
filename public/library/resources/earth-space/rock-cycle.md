# The Rock Cycle

Rocks continuously change among **three types** through heat, pressure, melting, and weathering. There is no fixed start — any rock can become any other.

**The three rock types**
- **Igneous** — forms when molten **magma** (underground) or **lava** (surface) cools and crystallizes. Examples: granite, basalt.
- **Sedimentary** — forms when **sediment** (weathered bits) is compacted and cemented in layers, or minerals precipitate. Examples: sandstone, limestone, shale.
- **Metamorphic** — forms when existing rock is changed by **heat and pressure** (without melting). Examples: marble (from limestone), slate (from shale), gneiss.

**The transforming processes**
- Weathering & erosion break rock into sediment.
- Compaction & cementation turn sediment into sedimentary rock.
- Heat & pressure turn any rock into metamorphic rock.
- Melting turns rock into magma; cooling & crystallization turn magma into igneous rock.

Driven by Earth's internal heat (mantle convection, plate tectonics) and surface processes (water, wind, ice).
