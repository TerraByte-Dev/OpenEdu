# Punctuation Marks

- **Period (.)** — ends a statement or command.
- **Question mark (?)** — ends a direct question.
- **Exclamation point (!)** — ends an emphatic or emotional statement.
- **Comma (,)** — separates items in a series, joins clauses with a conjunction, sets off introductory or nonessential parts.
- **Semicolon (;)** — joins two related independent clauses without a conjunction; separates list items that contain commas.
- **Colon (:)** — introduces a list, explanation, or quotation after a complete clause.
- **Apostrophe (')** — shows possession (*dog's*) or marks a contraction (*can't*). Plural possessive: *dogs'*.
- **Quotation marks (" ")** — enclose direct speech or a quoted phrase/title.
- **Parentheses ( )** — add extra, nonessential information.
- **Dash (—)** — sets off an abrupt break or emphasis; **hyphen (-)** joins compound words (*well-known*).
- **Ellipsis (…)** — marks an omission or a trailing-off pause.

Tip: use a **semicolon**, not a comma, to join two complete sentences without *and/but/or* (avoids a comma splice).
