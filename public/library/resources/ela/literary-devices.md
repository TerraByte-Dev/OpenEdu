# Literary Devices

Tools authors use to shape meaning beyond the literal words. (For comparisons and sound, see *figurative language*.)

- **Imagery** — vivid sensory description that paints a mental picture.
- **Symbolism** — an object/image that stands for a larger idea (a dove = peace).
- **Irony** — *verbal* (saying the opposite of what's meant), *situational* (outcome defies expectation), *dramatic* (reader knows what a character doesn't).
- **Foreshadowing** — hints at what will happen later.
- **Flashback** — an interruption that shows an earlier event.
- **Allusion** — a reference to another work, person, or event (often the Bible, myth, history).
- **Motif** — a recurring image or idea that supports the theme.
- **Theme** — the central message or insight about life.
- **Tone** — the author's attitude toward the subject; **mood** — the feeling created in the reader.
- **Characterization** — how a character is revealed (direct vs. indirect).
- **Point of view** — first person (*I*), second (*you*), or third (*he/she/they*, limited or omniscient).

Theme and tone are *inferred* from the other devices working together.
