# Comma Rules

Use a comma to:

1. **Separate items in a series:** *red, white, and blue.* (The final "Oxford" comma before *and* is standard in US academic writing.)
2. **Join two independent clauses** with a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS): *I ran, **but** I was late.*
3. **After an introductory word, phrase, or clause:** *After the game, we ate.* / *However, it rained.*
4. **Set off nonrestrictive (extra) information:** *My brother, who lives in Ohio, called.* (Don't use commas if the clause is essential.)
5. **Between coordinate adjectives:** *a long, winding road* (test: *and* fits between them).
6. **In dates, addresses, and large numbers:** *July 4, 1776* / *Atlanta, Georgia* / *1,000,000.*
7. **For direct address and dialogue tags:** *Maria, come here.* / *She said, "Hello."*

**Comma splice (error):** two complete sentences joined by only a comma — *It rained, we left.* Fix with a conjunction, a semicolon, or a period.
