# Parts of Speech

English words are sorted into **eight parts of speech** by the job a word does in a sentence.

- **Noun** — names a person, place, thing, or idea: *dog, Maria, city, freedom*.
- **Pronoun** — replaces a noun: *I, you, he, she, it, we, they, who, this*.
- **Verb** — shows action or a state of being: *run, think, is, become*.
- **Adjective** — describes a noun (which one? what kind? how many?): *red, tall, three, happy*.
- **Adverb** — describes a verb, adjective, or other adverb (how? when? where?), often ends *-ly*: *quickly, very, here, soon*.
- **Preposition** — shows a relationship of place or time: *in, on, under, before, with, between*.
- **Conjunction** — joins words, phrases, or clauses: *and, but, or, so, because* (FANBOYS = for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so).
- **Interjection** — expresses sudden emotion: *oh!, wow!, ouch!, hey!*.

A word's part of speech depends on its use: "**book** a flight" (verb) vs. "read a **book**" (noun). Some lists count *determiners/articles* (a, an, the) as a ninth class.
