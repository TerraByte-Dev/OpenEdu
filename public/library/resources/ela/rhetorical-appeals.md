# Rhetorical Appeals & Devices

**Rhetoric** is the art of persuasion. Aristotle named **three appeals** a speaker uses to convince an audience.

## The three appeals
- **Ethos** — appeal to **credibility / character**. The speaker is trustworthy, qualified, ethical. *"As a doctor of 20 years…"*
- **Pathos** — appeal to **emotion**. Stirs feelings (pity, fear, anger, hope) with vivid language or stories.
- **Logos** — appeal to **logic / reason**. Uses facts, data, evidence, and sound reasoning.

A fourth, **Kairos**, is the appeal to **timeliness** — making the argument at the right moment.

## Common rhetorical devices
- **Anaphora** — repeating a word/phrase at the start of clauses ("I have a dream…").
- **Antithesis** — contrasting ideas in parallel form ("ask not what your country can do for you…").
- **Parallelism** — repeating grammatical structure for rhythm.
- **Rhetorical question** — a question asked for effect, not an answer.
- **Allusion** — a reference to a known person, place, or work.
- **Hyperbole** — deliberate exaggeration for emphasis.
- **Repetition** — restating for emphasis.

## Logical fallacies (weak arguments to avoid)
Ad hominem (attack the person), straw man (misrepresent the argument), slippery slope, bandwagon, false dilemma, hasty generalization.
