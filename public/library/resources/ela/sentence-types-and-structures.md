# Sentence Types & Structures

Sentences are classified two ways: by **purpose** (what they do) and by **structure** (how their clauses combine).

## By purpose (and end mark)
| Type | Job | End mark |
|---|---|---|
| **Declarative** | makes a statement | . |
| **Interrogative** | asks a question | ? |
| **Imperative** | gives a command or request | . or ! |
| **Exclamatory** | shows strong emotion | ! |

## Clauses (the building blocks)
- **Independent clause** — a complete thought; can stand alone (*The dog barked*).
- **Dependent (subordinate) clause** — has a subject and verb but cannot stand alone (*because the dog barked*).

## By structure
| Structure | Made of |
|---|---|
| **Simple** | one independent clause |
| **Compound** | two+ independent clauses, joined by a comma + **FANBOYS** (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) or a semicolon |
| **Complex** | one independent + one or more dependent clauses |
| **Compound-complex** | two+ independent clauses + one or more dependent clauses |

*Simple:* She runs. *Compound:* She runs, **and** he walks. *Complex:* **When** the bell rang, she ran. *Compound-complex:* **When** the bell rang, she ran **and** he walked.
