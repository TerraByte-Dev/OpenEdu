# Point of View (POV)

**Point of view** is the perspective from which a story is told — who is narrating and how much they know.

| POV | Pronouns | The narrator… |
|---|---|---|
| **First person** | I, me, we | is a **character** in the story; we see only what they know. |
| **Second person** | you | addresses the reader as "you" (rare; common in how-to & choose-your-path). |
| **Third person limited** | he, she, they | is outside the story but follows **one** character's thoughts. |
| **Third person omniscient** | he, she, they | is "all-knowing" — reveals **any** character's thoughts and events anywhere. |
| **Third person objective** | he, she, they | reports only what is **observable** (actions, dialogue) — no inner thoughts. |

## Why it matters
- POV controls **what the reader knows** and **whom they trust**.
- A **reliable** narrator can be believed; an **unreliable** narrator (often first person) distorts or misleads.
- First person feels intimate but limited; third omniscient gives the big picture; third objective reads like a camera.

Don't confuse POV with **verb tense** (past/present) or with the author — the narrator is a *voice the author creates*.
